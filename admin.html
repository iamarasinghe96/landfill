2 — Kerbside Collection

▼
What: Total weight of general/residual waste from red (or dark-coloured) lid bins collected kerbside.

Source: Weighbridge records at the transfer station or direct from the collection contractor's monthly report.

Effect: Entirely sent to landfill — reducing this value improves the diversion rate. Typical range: 350–600 t/month for a council of this size.
What: Total weight of commingled recyclables (glass, plastics, metals, paper, cardboard) from yellow-lid bins.

Source: MRF (Materials Recovery Facility) monthly report or collection contractor.

Effect: A portion is diverted (net of contamination). Higher recycling with low contamination is optimal.
What: Percentage of the yellow-lid bin contents rejected at the MRF due to contamination (e.g., food scraps, nappies, plastic bags, garden waste).

Source: MRF monthly contamination report. Some MRFs provide a percentage; others report actual contamination tonnes.

Typical range: 10–25%. Australian average is ~18%. Lower is better.

Effect: Contaminated material is sent to landfill, reducing effective recycling tonnage.
What: Garden organics (grass clippings, leaves, branches, prunings) collected from lime/green-lid bins.

Source: Collection contractor monthly report or weighbridge at organics processing facility.

Effect: Processed into compost or mulch. After deducting organics rejection rate, the remaining portion counts as diverted. Key stream for improving overall rate.
What: Food organics collected separately (FOGO service). If AlburyCity does not run a FOGO service, enter 0.

Source: Collection contractor or organics processing facility monthly report.

Effect: Composted at an approved organics facility. Significantly boosts diversion rate — the NSW Government strongly encourages FOGO roll-out.

Note: FOGO includes fruit/vegetable scraps, meat, dairy, and food-soiled paper.
🌿

3 — Organics Processing

▼
What: The percentage of organic material received at the composting/processing facility that is rejected and sent to landfill due to contamination (e.g., plastics, glass in green waste).

Source: Organics processing facility monthly report. Also called "rejection rate" or "residue rate."

Typical range: 3–15%. Facilities processing FOGO typically have higher rejection rates (8–15%) than pure garden organics (3–8%).

Effect: Applied to the total organics collected (green waste + FOGO) to calculate the net diverted amount.
What: Actual weight of finished compost or mulch produced from the organics processing. This is for reporting purposes only — diversion is calculated from input tonnage minus rejection rate.

Note: Finished product is typically 40–60% lighter than input material due to moisture loss during composting. This field is informational and does not affect the diversion calculation.
Informational only — does not affect diversion calculation
🏭

4 — Transfer Station & Drop-off Facilities

▼
What: General waste received at the council's transfer station from residents (self-haul) that is not recovered and is sent to landfill.

Source: Transfer station weighbridge records. Exclude any material separated out for reuse or recycling (capture separately in "Materials Recovered").

Effect: Entirely sent to landfill. Reducing this through better education, reuse shops, and resource recovery facilities directly improves diversion.
What: Recoverable materials separated at the transfer station before waste goes to landfill. Includes timber, scrap metal, cardboard, clean concrete, bricks, and reusable items (Revolve/ReStore shop).

Source: Transfer station operator records or scrap metal contractor receipts.

Effect: Directly diverted from landfill. Every tonne recovered here improves the diversion rate.
What: Electronic waste (TVs, computers, phones, white goods, batteries, fluorescent lamps) collected at designated drop-off points or during hard waste collections.

Source: E-waste drop-off contractor's monthly report. Enter in kilograms.

Note: Under the National Television and Computer Recycling Scheme (NTCRS), approved products are collected free of charge. White goods and other items may have separate contractor arrangements.

Effect: Converted to tonnes internally (÷1000) for the diversion calculation.
What: Hazardous materials collected from residents — paints, chemicals, batteries (non-e-waste), fluorescent lamps, pesticides, motor oils, gas cylinders.

Source: HHW drop-off event records or permanent facility reports. Enter in kilograms.

Note: May be collected via periodic community events or permanent HHW drop-off stations. Data may need to be apportioned if events span multiple months.

Effect: Converted to tonnes internally (÷1000). Diverted from landfill and treated appropriately.
What: Mattresses, furniture, and other bulky items collected at the transfer station or during periodic kerbside clean-up events.

Source: Transfer station records or clean-up contractor report.

Note: Mattresses can be recycled through Soft Landing (national program). Furniture may be diverted through Revolve/op-shop networks.

Effect: Counted as diverted when recycled or reused. If the council cannot confirm diversion, set to 0 or exclude.
What: Used tyres collected from residents at the transfer station or designated drop-off.

Source: Transfer station records or Tyre Stewardship Australia (TSA) contractor receipts.

Note: Tyres are recycled under the TSA Product Stewardship scheme into crumb rubber, playground surfaces, and road materials. Council may receive reports quarterly — apportion monthly if needed.

Effect: Diverted from landfill. Tyres are banned from landfill in NSW.
🏗

5 — Construction & Demolition Waste

▼
What: Construction and demolition waste (concrete, bricks, timber, steel, plasterboard) that is recovered, recycled, or beneficially reused rather than landfilled.

Source: Recycling facility reports, transfer station records, or contractor data. May need to be obtained directly from the receiving C&D recycler.

Note: C&D waste is typically a large proportion of total waste volumes. High recovery rates (70–95%) are achievable with proper sorting. Does not include asbestos.

Effect: Major contributor to diversion. Focus on this stream for significant rate improvements.
What: Construction and demolition waste that cannot be recovered and is disposed of in landfill. Includes contaminated mixed C&D, asbestos (disposed separately at licensed facility), and non-recyclable residue.

Source: Receiving landfill weighbridge records. Must specify "C&D" waste category in the landfill receipt.

Note: Asbestos disposal should be tracked separately for safety reporting but included here for total waste accounting.
🏢

6 — Commercial & Industrial Waste

▼
What: Waste from businesses and commercial premises within the LGA that is diverted from landfill through recycling, composting, or resource recovery programs.

Source: Commercial waste contractor reports, council-operated commercial collection records, or waste audit data. This data can be difficult to obtain — use best available estimates with notes.

Note: Includes office paper, cardboard, food waste (if composted), and packaging recycling from commercial premises.

Effect: Counts toward the overall LGA diversion rate. Some reporting frameworks exclude C&I if council has no service responsibility.
What: Commercial and industrial waste generated within the LGA that is disposed of in landfill.

Source: Receiving landfill weighbridge records (filtered by LGA of origin) or commercial waste contractor reports. NSW EPA "Waste Data Portal" may have relevant aggregate data.

Note: If data is not available, enter 0 and note this in the Data Source field. Incomplete data reduces accuracy of the diversion rate for state reporting purposes.
♻️

7 — Other Diversions

▼
What: Unwanted clothing and textiles collected through charity donation bins, clothing exchanges, or council-facilitated collections.

Source: Charity partner reports (Vinnies, Salvos, etc.) or council textile collection program records.

Note: Only include textiles where diversion from landfill can be confirmed. Items going to charity shops for reuse or to textile recyclers both count as diverted.

Effect: Relatively small volume but easy diversion win. Growing as textile recycling infrastructure expands in Australia.
What: Glass containers collected separately from commingled recycling — through dedicated glass bins (purple lids), drop-off "igloos," or the Container Deposit Scheme (CDS/Return and Earn).

Source: Purple-bin contractor report, glass drop-off facility records, or CDS operator data.

Note: Separately collected glass achieves much higher recycling rates than glass in commingled bins (which often breaks and contaminates other materials). If your council runs purple bins, track this separately.

Effect: Counted as diverted. Also do not double-count with commingled recycling figures.
📊

8 — Service Context

▼
What: Total number of occupied residential dwellings receiving kerbside collection services from the council.

Source: Council rating records or collection contractor service data. Update annually or when service area changes.

Note: Used to calculate per-household waste generation (kg/household) which is often required for NSW EPA reporting and benchmark comparisons with other councils.
What: Estimated residential population within the LGA.

Source: ABS Census data (most recent) or DPIE population projections. Update every 1–2 years.

Note: Per-capita waste generation (kg/person/year) is a key state and national reporting metric. Albury's current population is approximately 56,000 (ABS 2021 Census). Growth projections suggest ~60,000 by 2030.
What: The council's overall diversion rate for the previous complete financial year. Used to calculate and display year-on-year improvement on the public dashboard.

Source: Annual Waste Report or previous year's final data submission to NSW EPA.

Note: This should be the full-year rate, not just the previous month. Update this figure once the full-year data has been finalised (typically August–September of the following year).

9 — Targets & Benchmarks

▼
What: Council's internal annual diversion rate target for the current financial year.

Source: AlburyCity Waste Strategy / Resource Recovery Strategy, or annual Operational Plan.

Note: Should increase progressively year-on-year. Align with the council's Sustainability Action Plan and NSW Government targets. Displaying this on the public dashboard helps community accountability.

Suggested trajectory: 50% by 2025, 55% by 2027, 65% by 2030.
What: The NSW Government's diversion/recovery target as set in the Waste and Sustainable Materials Strategy 2041 (WaSM Strategy).

Current targets:
• 70% recovery of all materials by 2025 (interim)
• 80% by 2030
• 90% by 2041

Source: NSW EPA / Department of Climate Change, Energy, the Environment and Water (DCCEEW) policy documents.

Note: Update if state government revises targets. The 2025 interim target of 70% applies to all material types combined.
The year by which the NSW Government target should be achieved. This is displayed on the public dashboard to give context to the target progress bar. Update if the target or its timeline changes.
